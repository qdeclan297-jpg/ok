# Part 5: £300 a month, UK, long-term growth

£300/month in pounds changes the answer from the previous parts. For a UK
investor the **wrapper you use is worth far more than the fund you pick**, and
there are two options that beat every investment decision on the table because
they hand you free money before any market return.

Numbers reproduce with `research/07_uk_300_per_month.py`.

---

## 1. What £300/month actually becomes

| Years | Paid in | @4%/yr | @6%/yr | @8%/yr | @10%/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | £18,000 | £19,854 | £20,846 | £21,883 | £22,968 |
| 10 | £36,000 | £44,009 | £48,742 | £54,037 | £59,959 |
| 20 | £72,000 | £109,153 | £136,032 | £170,700 | £215,478 |
| 30 | £108,000 | £205,581 | £292,354 | **£422,565** | £618,853 |
| 35 | £126,000 | £269,975 | £412,081 | £642,770 | £1,019,637 |

4% is your savings account. 8% is roughly what global equities have delivered
long run. **At 30 years that difference is £217,000** — and it is the entire
argument for investing rather than saving.

Note how little separates the columns at 5 years and how much at 30. Compounding
does almost nothing early and almost everything late. That is why the single most
valuable thing you have here is *time*, not fund selection.

## 2. The priority order — this matters more than the fund

### First: workplace pension, if your employer matches

If your employer matches contributions, nothing else comes close. Via salary
sacrifice a basic-rate taxpayer gives up **72p of take-home to put £1 in** (20%
income tax + 8% NI saved). Then the employer match doubles the matched portion.

| Route | £300 of your take-home becomes | 30 yrs @8% |
|---|---|---|
| ISA | £300 invested | £422,565 |
| Pension, no match | ~£417 invested | £587,366 |
| **Pension, employer matches** | **~£834 invested** | **£1,174,731** |

That is not a better investment — it is the *same* investment with free money
attached. If you are not taking a full employer match, that is the first thing
to fix, ahead of every other decision in this document.

**The catch:** locked until age 57–58, and taxable on the way out beyond the 25%
tax-free lump sum. It is retirement money, not general savings.

### Second: Lifetime ISA, if you're eligible

Open between 18 and 39, contribute until 50. The government adds **25% on every
pound**, up to £1,000/year on £4,000 contributed. Your £3,600/year sits under
that cap, so **you'd get the full £900/year**.

| Years | £300/mo into ISA | £300/mo into LISA | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | £54,037 | £67,547 | +£13,509 |
| 20 | £170,700 | £213,375 | +£42,675 |
| 30 | £422,565 | **£528,206** | **+£105,641** |

A guaranteed 25% before any market return. No investment on this list offers
that.

**The catches, and they are real:**
- Penalty-free only for a **first home under £450,000**, or from **age 60**.
- Any other withdrawal costs a **25% penalty**, which takes back more than the
  bonus gave — you end up below what you put in.
- Account must be open 12 months before a house purchase.
- **The LISA is being replaced by a First-Time Buyer ISA in April 2028**, so if
  this route appeals, the window to open one is limited.

### Third: Stocks & Shares ISA

£20,000 allowance for 2026/27; your £3,600 fits easily. Completely tax-free
growth, tax-free withdrawals, accessible any time, no penalties.

Over 30 years at 8% you'd pay in £108,000 and gain £314,565. **Inside an ISA
that gain is untaxed.** Outside one, up to £75,496 of it could be exposed to
capital gains tax at 24%.

> **The ISA wrapper is worth more than any fund choice in this document.** Use it
> before optimising anything else.

At £300/month, percentage platform fees barely matter but **flat fees are
brutal**. Vanguard UK charges £4/month below £32,000 — that's £48 on £3,600 in
year one, or **1.33%**.

**Spread between best and worst: £38,714.** Free is free — take it.

## 4. The fund

Fee competition has been fierce, so check current figures before buying — several
of these changed in 2026.

**All-World (developed + emerging markets, ~3,800 stocks):**

| Fund | Ticker | OCF |
|---|---|---|
| HSBC FTSE All World Index | — | 0.13% |
| Vanguard FTSE All-World | VWRP | ~0.14% (cut from 0.19% in July 2026) |
| iShares MSCI ACWI | SSAC | 0.20% |

**Developed markets only (no EM, ~1,300–1,500 stocks):**

| Fund | Ticker | OCF |
|---|---|---|
| Invesco MSCI World | MXWO | **0.05%** |
| UBS Core MSCI World | WRDA | 0.06% |
| Fidelity Index World | — | 0.12% |

The 0.09% gap between Invesco's 0.05% and VWRP's 0.14% is worth roughly £10,000
over 30 years here. Real, but an order of magnitude less than the wrapper
decisions above.

What you give up at 0.05% is emerging markets — about 10% of the world by market
cap. Part 4 of this research found EM has badly underperformed and correlates
+0.863 with world equity anyway, so the practical difference is small. Holding
All-World is the neutral position that requires no view; holding developed-only
is slightly cheaper and implicitly bets EM keeps lagging.

**Either is defensible. Pick one, buy it monthly, don't switch.** Switching
between them repeatedly would cost more than the fee difference ever saves.

Choose an **accumulating** share class (dividends reinvested automatically —
nothing to do, nothing to reinvest) and note these are **Irish-domiciled UCITS**,
which as Part 3 covered avoids the US estate-tax exposure that US-listed ETFs
carry for non-US investors.

## 5. Putting it together

For £300/month aimed at long-term growth, in order:

1. **Check your workplace pension match.** If there's unclaimed employer match,
   redirect there first. Free money, ~£750k difference over 30 years.
2. **If you're under 40 and might buy a first home, or want retirement money at
   60:** a LISA gives a guaranteed +25%. Worth ~£105k over 30 years. Understand
   the withdrawal penalty first, and note it closes to new savers in 2028.
3. **Otherwise, or alongside:** Stocks & Shares ISA on a zero-fee platform
   (Trading 212 or InvestEngine), one global index fund, accumulating, set up as
   an automatic monthly purchase.
4. **Then leave it alone.** The 30-year column only happens if you don't
   interrupt it.

The boring answer, one more time, is the one that won every test across five
studies: **one global fund, lowest possible fees, inside a tax wrapper, bought
automatically every month, held for decades.**

## 6. What I can't tell you

- **Whether your employer matches, and at what rate.** This is the single
  highest-value unknown here. Check your payslip or HR portal.
- **Your age**, which determines LISA eligibility entirely.
- **Whether you'll need this money sooner.** Everything above assumes you won't
  touch it. If you might need it within ~5 years, the 4% savings account is the
  right home — Part 3 showed 27% of single years in the S&P 500 lost money.
- **Your tax band**, debts, or emergency fund. High-interest debt beats every
  return here. Most guidance suggests 3–6 months of expenses in accessible cash
  before investing at all.
- **Whether the next 30 years resemble the last 30.** Every figure is historical.

I'm not a financial adviser and this is research, not a personal recommendation.
For a decision this long-dated, the UK's free [MoneyHelper](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk)
service is a reasonable sanity check, and regulated advice is worth it if your
situation is complicated.
